NAME
       etai - appointment scheduling for contractors and clients
       through messaging

OVERVIEW
       ETAi is a system where clients schedule, reschedule, and
       cancel appointments with contractors entirely through
       messages. There is no chat interface and no app for the
       client to learn. The client texts, the system books.

       The system has three main parts, plus a UI:

       1. msg   - the messaging service
       2. bus   - the central router
       3. cal   - the calendar service and its AI agent
       4. ui    - the video calling platform

PARTS

   msg
       The messaging service. It owns all inbound and outbound
       channels: SMS, iMessage, phone calls, and video calls.
       Both contractors and clients talk to the system through
       msg. It holds no scheduling logic. Everything it receives
       goes to the bus. Everything it sends comes from the bus.

   bus
       The central router. Every message in the system passes
       through the bus. Nothing talks point to point.

       For now the bus is a plain forwarder. Inbound traffic
       from msg is forwarded to the calendar AI agent through
       the cal API. Inbound traffic from cal (notifications,
       confirmations, questions back to users) is forwarded to
       msg for delivery.

       In the future the bus earns its keep: it can summarize a
       conversation before forwarding, enrich a request with
       weather or travel time, and fan a message out to both
       msg and cal. The forwarding contract stays the same;
       the bus just gets smarter about what it sends.

   cal
       The calendar service. It stores events: location, time,
       name, and the original requester. It contains its own AI
       agent, exposed through an API.

       We lean on the cal AI as much as possible. All natural
       language interpretation, scheduling decisions, conflict
       handling, and rescheduling live there. The bus does not
       parse intent. msg does not parse intent. If a message
       needs understanding, it goes to cal.

   ui
       A video calling platform. Contractors and clients can
       talk over video. The ui sends prose (transcripts,
       summaries of what was said) back to the bus, and the bus
       forwards that prose to the cal AI, which creates or
       updates events from it just like a text message.

USERS
       Contractors are the people who travel to job sites.
       Clients are the people who contact the system to
       schedule, reschedule, or cancel appointments.

       Contractors get a daily summary of their schedule in the
       message channel of their choice.

       Clients get notifications when the contractor is on
       site, when the contractor is running late, and when an
       appointment needs to be cancelled or rescheduled.

DATA FLOW
       Inbound:

           client/contractor -> msg -> bus -> cal AI

       Outbound (notifications, confirmations):

           cal -> bus -> msg -> client/contractor

       Video:

           ui -> bus -> cal AI

       All messages go through the bus. There are no direct
       connections between msg, cal, and ui.

FUTURE
       Weather and location integration. Contractors will know
       when they need to leave for the next job. Clients will
       know how far away their contractor is. The bus is where
       this enrichment happens: it can attach weather and
       travel time to a request before it reaches cal, or
       summarize and send to both parts at once.

       Other stretch goals live in the README: iMessage, AI
       voice on phone calls, an Uber-style client UI with ETA
       and location, parts ordering, and meeting
       prioritization.

GOTCHAS
       All messages go through the bus. Do not wire msg to cal
       directly, even when it looks shorter. The bus is the
       only place that sees the whole system, which is what
       lets it add enrichment later without touching the
       services.

       Scheduling intelligence lives in cal, not the bus. The
       moment the bus starts parsing intent it becomes a
       second agent and the two will disagree. Keep the bus
       dumb about meaning; let it be smart about routing and
       enrichment only.

       msg is a transport, not a product surface. Formatting a
       message for SMS vs iMessage is msg's job; deciding what
       to say is cal's job; deciding where it goes is the
       bus's job.

TRIED APPROACHES
       Smart bus from day one: rejected. Letting the bus parse
       intent and decide what cal should do duplicates the cal
       AI and gives us two sources of scheduling truth. The
       plain forwarder keeps one agent in charge while we
       learn what enrichment the bus actually needs.

       Direct msg -> cal connection: rejected. It saves a hop
       now but means notifications, video prose, and future
       weather enrichment each need their own wiring. One
       router, one place to change.
